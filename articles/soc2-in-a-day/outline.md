## Argument in one sentence

"SOC 2 compliance is a revenue gate that prices out small startups — but the compliance knowledge,
not the tooling, is the real barrier, and an AI-assisted practitioner can collapse months of
consulting work into a day by encoding domain expertise into agentic workflows."

## Objections to address

The counterarguments the idea lists, and how each is handled:

1. "DIY compliance is risky — you'll miss something an auditor catches" — acknowledge directly. The
   kit doesn't replace the auditor; it replaces the $50K-$100K of readiness consulting that happens
   *before* the audit. Frame the kit as audit preparation, not audit replacement. Handled in the
   counterargument section.
2. "AI-generated policies won't hold up under audit scrutiny" — the policies are generated by an AI
   executing on compliance domain knowledge from a former GDPO. "The AI implements; the human
   architects." Handled in the counterargument section.
3. "Open-source compliance tools can't keep up with framework changes" — SOC 2 trust criteria change
   infrequently (last major revision was 2017); the evidence gathering and policy templates are the
   parts that need updating, and the markdown + MCP architecture makes updates straightforward.
4. "If you need SOC 2, you can afford $15K for a platform" — not true for pre-revenue startups
   trying to land their first enterprise contract. The SOC 2 requirement often comes before the
   revenue that would fund the compliance spend — the chicken-and-egg problem.
5. "This can't possibly scale to a real company" — correct, and the piece should say so explicitly.
   The author runs SOC 2 for larger organizations and uses Vanta for those. "Acknowledging the
   ceiling makes the recommendation honest and builds trust with the CTO audience who knows better."
