## Role of research and thought leadership in the content mix

This repository produces one thing: a researched long-form argument with imagery. `WORKFLOW.md`
principle 2 sets the bar for what earns publication — "an argument, synthesis, first-hand
experience, new evidence, or a useful framework" — and principle 3 requires sourced facts,
interpretation and opinion to stay separable. The mix question is therefore about which *kind* of
contribution each month's articles make, not about volume.

Carried across, **needs confirmation**: `channel-strategy.md` (2026-04-11) records a monthly
long-form content mix for the newsletter:

| Per month | Pillar | Kind of contribution this implies |
|---|---|---|
| 2× | Field Notes (`field_notes`) | First-hand practitioner evidence — retrospectives, failures, tool use |
| 2× | The Shift (`the_shift`) | Synthesis and interpretation of industry change |
| 1× | The Stack (`the_stack`) | Technical argument or decision record |
| 1× | The On-Ramp (`the_onramp`) | Explanation and framework for readers who have not started |

So four of six slots per month (Field Notes and The Stack) are first-hand practitioner work, and two
are interpretive. That balance — practitioner-heavy, with analysis grounded in things the author
actually built — is consistent with the standing recorded in `brand/positioning.md`.

Two cautions on that table:

- It is 5 months old and describes six long-form pieces a month, which is a heavier load than the
  recorded publication history supports: `background/published-work.md` records seven long-form
  articles across roughly eight weeks, then nothing after 2026-04-10.
- It disagrees with the mix recorded in `background/topic-clusters.md`, which reports the source
  material setting a **weekly** pillar balance of The Shift 2×, The Stack 2×, Field Notes 1–2×, The
  On-Ramp 1×, and Alberta Angle 1× when relevant. The two records differ in both period and
  per-pillar weight. **`To decide` — which mix, if either, still stands.** Do not reconcile them by
  guessing.

## Success measures

These are the author's own stated targets, carried with their sources and dates. Actual results are
not recorded in this repository at all — measurement, reporting and performance review live in the
tracking project.

From `substack-growth-strategy.md` (2026-03-13):

| Measure | Stated target |
|---|---|
| Free subscriber growth | 5%+ month-over-month |
| Open rate | 40%+ healthy; flag below 30% |
| Subscriber source mix | Shifting toward Substack-internal sources |

From `channel-strategy.md` (2026-04-11), for the Substack newsletter:

| Measure | Stated target |
|---|---|
| Open rate | >50% |
| Click rate | >10% |
| Subscriber growth | 10%/month |

**The two documents disagree, and this document does not reconcile them.** The open-rate target is
40%+ in the newer-diagnosed growth document (2026-03-13) and >50% in the channel document
(2026-04-11); subscriber growth is 5%+ per month in the first and 10% per month in the second. The
conflicting pairs are recorded side by side deliberately. **`To decide` — which open-rate target and
which growth target stand**, and whether either survives a publication gap since 2026-04-10.

How a measure would inform an editorial decision, so these numbers are not decoration:

- A sustained **open rate** below the flag threshold is a signal about subject lines, sending
  rhythm, and whether the promised reader problem is recognisable — a commissioning and framing
  question for `brief.md`, not a reason to publish more.
- **Click rate** speaks to whether an article's desired reader action is real and stated; if
  articles ask for nothing, the measure has nothing to report.
- **Subscriber growth** and the **source mix** are distribution measures, not article-quality
  measures. Acting on them changes `strategy/channels.md` and `strategy/distribution-playbook.md`,
  not the editorial bar.
- **`To decide` — whether any editorial measure exists at all.** Every target above is a newsletter
  performance metric. Nothing recorded measures whether an argument landed, was cited, or changed a
  reader's decision.
